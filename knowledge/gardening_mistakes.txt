Common Beginner Gardening Mistakes

Beginners often encounter problems because they provide too much or too little water, choose unsuitable plants, overlook sunlight requirements, or make changes without observing the plant's response.

Common mistakes include:
- Overwatering plants
- Underwatering plants
- Using containers without drainage
- Choosing plants without checking sunlight requirements
- Planting too closely together
- Using unsuitable soil
- Applying too much fertilizer
- Ignoring pests
- Transplanting carelessly
- Pruning at the wrong time
- Starting too many plants at once
- Ignoring seasonal conditions
- Placing plants in unsuitable locations
- Failing to monitor changes in leaves and stems

Overwatering is a common problem. More water does not automatically mean healthier plants. Water requirements depend on plant type, soil, container, weather, and growing conditions.

Poor drainage can cause water to remain around roots for too long. Containers should have suitable drainage and soil should match the plant.

Plant spacing matters because overcrowding can affect access to light and air and make maintenance more difficult.

Beginners should observe symptoms rather than immediately assuming the cause. Yellow leaves, wilting, slow growth, leaf spots, or damaged leaves can have multiple possible causes.
